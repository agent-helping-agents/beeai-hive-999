# FINITE AND INFINITE GAMES - James P. Carse

## 📖 Book Information
- **Author:** James P. Carse (Religious studies scholar)
- **Publisher:** Free Press
- **Publication Date:** 1986
- **ISBN:** 978-0-02-905980-0
- **Available:** Internet Archive (archive.org/details/finiteinfinitega00carsrich)

## 🎯 Core Thesis
There are two kinds of games: finite and infinite. A finite game is played for the purpose of winning; an infinite game is played for the purpose of continuing the play.

## 📝 Key Concepts

### Finite Games
- **Definition:** Games with fixed rules, clear boundaries, and announced winners/losers
- **Examples:** Sports, politics, wars, business competitions
- **Characteristics:**
  - Theatrical (performed for an audience)
  - Seek power through victory
  - Have definite endings
  - Require compliance with rules

### Infinite Games
- **Definition:** Games played to continue playing, with changing rules and transcended boundaries
- **Examples:** Culture, religion, philosophy, love, life itself
- **Characteristics:**
  - Dramatic (enacted in the present)
  - Display self-sufficient strength
  - No definite ending
  - Play with boundaries, change rules

### Theatrical vs. Dramatic
- **Theatrical:** Following a script, performing for others, seeking approval
- **Dramatic:** Living authentically in the present, creating meaning through action

## 💡 Major Themes

1. **Power vs. Strength**
   - Finite players seek power (over others)
   - Infinite players demonstrate strength (self-sufficiency)

2. **Scripted vs. Emergent**
   - Finite games follow predetermined scripts
   - Infinite games create new possibilities as they unfold

3. **Audience vs. Participants**
   - Finite games need witnesses/audiences
   - Infinite games involve authentic participants

4. **Boundaries**
   - Finite games have fixed boundaries
   - Infinite games expand and transcend boundaries
